        How it works

        The checker applies 49 hand-written rules in 7 families (sentence starters, connectives and punctuation, particles and pronouns, register, English calques, word order, and text statistics). Each rule turns what it finds into a log-likelihood ratio whose parameters were typed in by hand from training-split counts; family sums are capped and added up to a score, where above zero means “translated”. There is no machine learning, no tagger and no parser, so every point of the score can be traced to a rule and, mostly, to words in the text. The rules were calibrated on European Parliament debates (Europarl, 1997–2004): Swedish speeches versus speeches given in English and translated into Swedish by the Parliament, so other genres, later Swedish and machine translation may behave differently. This page runs a JavaScript port of the Python engine that was checked to give the same scores, clues and contributions on 2,782 test texts.
